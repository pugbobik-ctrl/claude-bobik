# Round 2 references: mechanisms for a surprising place card

Status key: [opened] = I fetched the page and read it. [search-only] = seen only in a search result snippet, page not opened (or fetch blocked, 403). [unverified] = detail I could not confirm at all.
Brief: black print on lemon card, Wix Madefor Text, small black cone lamps on tables, must not echo blobby lettering / receipt runners.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
## A. SHADOW TYPOGRAPHY / SHADOW ART

### 1. Fred Eerdekens (BE, b. 1951): "Untitled" (1991); "Short Story" (c. 2006); "Some Need"
- How it works: a tangle of bent copper wire or a metal frame looks like abstract squiggles. One lamp (or a candle) at a fixed angle throws a shadow on the wall that is clear, legible text. In "Untitled" (1991) two lights on opposite sides give two different words from the same object ("ADMIRE" / "REJECT"). Small twisted copper pieces over lit candles spell a full sentence.
- To a place card: a die-cut or bent-wire/strip name object that looks abstract and throws the guest's name when a lamp or tealight hits it from a set angle. Two-sided variant: one side lit gives the name, the other side gives a second word.
- Lamp caveat: a cone-shaded lamp throws light mainly down and out over the table, so the shadow lands on the tablecloth and not on a wall. The card must be designed for a low raking light and a table-surface shadow, or ship with a pocket tealight or LED. This is untested in the real room, so mock up under the actual lamp. [unverified for this lamp]
- Sources: https://www.moca.london/fredeerdekensuntitled.html [opened]; https://coolhunting.com/culture/fred-eerdekens/ [opened]

### 2. Shigeo Fukuda (JP, 1932-2009): "Lunch with a Helmet On" (1987)
- How it works: a heap of forks, knives and spoons welded together. Lit from the right angle, the shadow is a detailed motorcycle. It is shadow art made from tableware, so it fits a dinner thematically.
- To a place card: the card itself is cut or bent into a small heap or stack of slats, and the table lamp resolves the stack into a single silhouette (a fork, a glass, a lemon). Cutlery-as-material is a witty link to the DNA Kitchen food side.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeo_Fukuda [opened] (the Wikipedia page confirms 1987 and the cutlery/motorcycle shadow; museum page not found).

### 3. Tim Noble & Sue Webster: "Dirty White Trash (with Gulls)" (1998)
- How it works: six months of their rubbish plus taxidermy gulls, heaped. A single slide projector throws a shadow that is a double self-portrait of the artists with wine and cigarette. Chaos in the object, order in the shadow.
- To a place card: a deliberately messy tuft of cut paper offcuts that resolves into a name or a portrait silhouette under the lamp. Needs a fixed lamp angle, so treat it as a table-centre piece rather than per-seat. Idea only.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Noble_and_Sue_Webster [search-only]

### 4. Hans-Peter Feldmann: "Schattenspiel / Shadow Play" (c. 2002-04; dates vary by source)
- How it works: found toys and trinkets on slowly rotating turntables under spotlights. The shadows weave into one another and form changing compound figures on the wall. The turntables, cables and lamps are left visible.
- To a place card: a small cut figure on a pin or spindle that the guest spins with a finger; its shadow under the cone lamp morphs as it turns. The mechanism is exposed and unpolished, which is honest and un-slick.
- Source: https://arnolfini.org.uk/?p=25801 [opened] (does not give the year; the 2002 vs 2004 variation is from a search snippet).

### 5. Bruno Munari: "Macchine inutili" (Useless Machines, 1930s onward; exhibited 1934-1987) and "Concavo-convesso"
- How it works: hanging structures of card, thread and light materials that move on the slightest air current and throw changing shadows on the wall. The later "Concavo-convesso" are folded wire-mesh sculptures that produce "evanescent images" when lit. Munari himself used the idea of combining chance with a programmed form.
- To a place card: a card with a small hung element (a cut tag on a thread) that sways with warm air from a candle and breathing at the table. A kinetic detail that is not mechanical. (Munari's "Sculture da viaggio" is already covered; this is a different work.)
- Sources: https://kaufmannrepetto.com/exhibition/macchine-inutili-useless-machines-1934-1987/ [opened]; https://www.arshake.com/en/bruno-munaris-useless-machines-part-iv/ [opened]

## B. ANAMORPHOSIS / VIEWPOINT-DEPENDENT READING

### 6. Markus Raetz (CH, 1941-2020): "Oui-Non" (2000, Geneva); "Yes/No" (reported as 2003 by one search source); "Dutch Window" (1996-98)
- How it works: a bent metal line or strip. Seen from one side the twists spell "yes", walk around it and the same three twists read "no". "Dutch Window" is a blocky window lintel that reads "iets" (something) from one viewpoint and "niets" (nothing) from another.
- To a place card: a single bent, folded or cut strip that reads the guest's first name from their own seat and a second word (the neighbour's name, "Dinner", a course, a joke) from across the table. A dinner table is a natural set of fixed viewpoints, so this suits the setting very well. Mechanism: a twisted ribbon of two-sided lettering; the letter outlines are cut as ruled surfaces.
- Sources: https://www.mirabaud.com/en/contemporary-art/artists/artist/markus-raetz [opened; confirms Oui-Non 2000]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Raetz [opened]. The "Dutch Window" and "Yes/No 2003" details come from search snippets only [search-only].

### 7. Felice Varini (CH, b. 1952): e.g. "Ellipse noire Paris" (2008)
- How it works: a geometric shape painted across walls, floors and objects at different distances. It reads as one clean shape only from a single marked vantage point; from anywhere else it fragments into abstract strokes.
- To a place card: a seated-eye-height anamorphosis. A flat card, or a card with a stepped or slanted profile, carries type that is stretched along the line of sight so it reads correctly only from the seated guest's eye position (a known, fixed height and distance at a table). Standing over it, the word is a smear. Mark the vantage with a small notch in the table edge.
- Source: https://semiose.com/en/artist/felice-varini/ [opened]

### 8. Romain Oederlin (ECAL), "Archetype" (2023 diploma project)
- How it works: 3D-designed typefaces whose legibility changes with point of view, flickering between letter and abstract form.
- To a place card: a type-specific precedent for a cut-and-folded 3D letter structure that you resolve by leaning. Useful as a design-school reference for the typographic treatment.
- Source: https://ecal.ch/en/feed/projects/7538/archetype/ [opened]

### 9. István Orosz (HU, b. 1951): catoptric anamorphs, e.g. "Mysterious Island" (Jules Verne portrait)
- How it works: a flat print shows a distorted image; a polished cylinder or cone placed on a marked circle reflects it as a normal image.
- To a place card: a print on the card that is nonsense until the guest sets a glass, a polished spoon or a tin cylinder on it. The guest's own tableware completes the work. Nice because it uses the table kit; risky because mirror cylinders are not guaranteed.
- Sources: https://amusingplanet.com/2010/04/anamorphic-art-by-istvan-orosz.html [search-only]; NGA object page https://www.nga.gov/collection/art-object-page.223616.html (403 on fetch) [search-only]

### 10. Kokichi Sugihara (JP, Meiji University): "Ambiguous Cylinder Illusion" (2016, 2nd prize Best Illusion of the Year)
- How it works: objects that look like rectangular-section cylinders directly look circular-section in a mirror, though they are the same objects. The brain cannot reconcile them even when the object is rotated.
- To a place card: one card standing in front of a small mirror (or a mirrored strip) gives a different shape or message. Heavy to engineer (needs exact 3D geometry), so more of a hero piece than a per-seat piece.
- Source: https://illusionoftheyear.com/2016/06/ambiguous-cylinder-illusion/ [opened]

## C. ROCKING / BALANCING / SELF-RIGHTING

### 11. Ellsworth Kelly: "Rocker" works (concept from 1959; "Blue Red Rocker" 1963; "Untitled" 1982)
- How it works: a folded ellipse of sheet material that rocks on its curved edge. Kelly got the idea by folding a coffee-cup lid in half and rocking it on a table. (Kelly "Totems" are already covered; this is a different series and the brief named it.)
- To a place card: a folded ellipse of lemon card with a curved rim that wobbles when nudged and settles; printed name on the inside curve. Cheap to cut and instant to understand.
- Source: https://www.slam.org/blog/curator-qa-ellsworth-kelly/ [opened]

### 12. Gabor Domokos & Peter Varkonyi: Gomboc (shape proved to exist 2006; first physical model 2007)
- How it works: a convex homogeneous body with exactly one stable and one unstable equilibrium point; wherever it is set down it rolls back upright (a self-righting shape, like a tortoise shell).
- To a place card: a paper or card shell whose geometry makes the name face the guest after every wobble. Strict Gomboc geometry is hard (tolerance about 1/10,000 of size); a weighted-base roly-poly gets the same behaviour with a coin or a clay weight. Reference value is the story and the mathematics.
- Source: https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Gomboc.html [opened]

### 13. Okiagari-koboshi / Daruma (Japan; papier-mache tumbler, 17th century or earlier)
- How it works: hollow papier-mache body weighted at the bottom; it springs back upright when knocked, a symbol of resilience. Traditionally a good-luck charm.
- To a place card: a small hollow card or papier-mache egg/cone with a weighted foot. A knock-it-and-it-returns behaviour, with a gift-ready cultural meaning. A cone shape also echoes the table lamps. [search-only]
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daruma_doll [search-only]

### 14. Peter Fischli & David Weiss: "Quiet Afternoon / Equilibres" (1984-85) and "The Way Things Go" (1987)
- How it works: everyday household objects balanced precariously on and in each other, photographed at the edge of collapse. They later turned the collapses into a chain reaction film.
- To a place card: card, cutlery and glass balanced as a small precarious stack; or a row of cards where nudging one starts the next. Conceptual references rather than a production recipe.
- Source: https://www.tate.org.uk/whats-on/tate-modern/exhibition/fischli-weiss [opened]

## D. SPINNING / FLIP / LAYER REVEALS / FLEXAGONS

### 15. Marcel Duchamp: "Rotoreliefs (Optical Discs)" (1935)
- How it works: six printed discs, 20 cm, spiral designs on both sides; placed on a turntable at about 33 rpm they appear to have depth. Looking with one eye strengthens the effect; Duchamp's box had a hole in the lid for one-eyed viewing. A commercial product that failed at the 1935 Concours Lepine, reissued 1953/59/63.
- To a place card: a disc card with an off-centre or spiral cut pattern that sits on a pin or a flat dimple; a finger spin gives the depth illusion and then stops on the guest's name. The lid hole idea transfers directly.
- Source: https://www.boijmans.nl/en/collection/artworks/85583/rotoreliefs-optical-disks [opened]

### 16. Thaumatrope (John Ayrton Paris, 1825)
- How it works: a disc on two strings with a different image on each side; spun, the images fuse (bird + cage).
- To a place card: a disc, name on one face and a graphic on the other (a lemon + a knife, or two halves of a word), strung through two holes. Twist the strings and the halves fuse into the full word. Very cheap, very legible.
- Source: https://graphicarts.princeton.edu/2015/10/08/persistence-of-vision [opened]

### 17. Hexaflexagon (Arthur H. Stone, Princeton 1939; Martin Gardner, Scientific American, Dec 1956)
- How it works: a strip of 19 triangles folded flat into a hexagon; pinch and flex and it turns inside out to expose hidden faces (six in the hexahexaflexagon, found by the "Tuckerman traverse").
- To a place card: a flat hexagon place card where each of the faces holds different content (name, menu course, the artist, a question for the table). The guest discovers faces by flexing it. A 1959 patent already named advertising display as a use; artists have used flexagons as book structures. Lemon + black hexagon is graphically strong and does not echo the poster.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexagon [opened]

### 18. Yaacov Agam: "Agamographs" (from 1953 "polyphonic paintings"; "Complex Vision" 1969) and Carlos Cruz-Diez: "Physichromie" (e.g. No. 226, 1966)
- How it works: Agam: a zigzag/accordion surface or lenticular screen where each facet carries a different image, so the picture changes with viewing angle. Cruz-Diez: thin strips of metal and coloured plastic stripes over painted lines make a moire-like effect with colour that shifts with movement and light.
- To a place card: a pleated (accordion-folded) card with name A on the left facets and name B (or "Dinner") on the right facets; printed on the sides of the pleats only. Black on lemon works. Reads as a flat abstract stripe pattern face-on. Cheap, no electronics.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaacov_Agam [opened]; https://imma.ie/collection/physichromie-no-226/ [opened]

### 19. Rufus Butler Seder: "Scanimation" (patented 2006; barrier-grid animation from 1890s)
- How it works: an interlaced image under a striped transparent overlay; moving the overlay shows one frame at a time, so the image animates.
- To a place card: a cut-slotted stripe card (the card itself is the grid) over a printed underlay; sliding a tab changes the shown image or word. Printing needs pixel-precise registration (needs a test on the real printer).
- Source: https://www.opticalillusion.net/optical-illusions/animated-moire-or-scanimation/ [search-only]

## E. KIRIGAMI / DEPLOYABLE / CHAIN / MODULAR

### 20. Ahmad Rafsanjani & Katia Bertoldi: "Buckling-Induced Kirigami" (Physical Review Letters 118, 2017)
- How it works: a thin sheet with a square array of orthogonal cuts leaves squares joined by small ligaments. Under tension the ligaments buckle out of plane and the flat sheet forms 3D patterns (the pattern depends on load direction). Stretched far enough, plastic strain leaves permanent folds.
- To a place card: a flat lemon card with a cut lattice and the name in the uncut border. The guest pulls two ends and the middle erupts into a textured, light-catching 3D field (also lets lamp light through). Test on thin 200-250 gsm card; card tears more readily than polymer sheet.
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06470 [opened]

### 21. Ronan & Erwan Bouroullec: "Algues" (2004, Vitra)
- How it works: injection-moulded seaweed-like plastic pieces (320 x 257 x 40 mm) that attach to one another at multiple points; the user builds screens or canopies and chooses density.
- To a place card: each card is one "alga" with a tab at each end that clips into the neighbour's tab; seated guests together build one chain or screen along the table and the pattern is only complete when all are seated. Needs symmetrical tab geometry and enough tolerance for paper.
- Sources: MoMA page https://www.moma.org/collection/works/98628 (403 on fetch) [search-only]; https://bouroullec.com/view_projets_detail_new.php?id=82 [search-only]

### 22. Kenneth Snelson (tensegrity patent 1965; term from Buckminster Fuller)
- How it works: rigid struts floating in a continuous network of tension cables; compression and tension are separated.
- To a place card: a small card-and-thread tensegrity (three card struts, thread) that stands on its own. Hard to build at hundreds of units; keep as a hero piece or reference only. [search-only]
- Source: web.mit.edu tensegrity page (https://web.mit.edu/hmsallum/www/portfolio/tensegrity/tensegritypage.html) [search-only]

## F. LIGHT / TRANSLUCENCY / ARTISTS' BOOKS / PACKAGING

### 23. Ingo Maurer: "Zettel'z 5" (1997)
- How it works: a pendant lamp whose shade is 80 sheets of Japanese paper clipped on steel rods; 31 carry printed poems, letters and sketches, 49 are blank for the owner. The notes cast different strengths of shadow, so writing becomes light.
- To a place card: a translucent vellum or rice-paper card with text on its back, meant to be stood close to the lamp so the text appears in the glow, or clipped on the lamp shade. Cone lamps are likely opaque black, so this needs a tealight or an LED. [unverified for black cone lamps]
- Sources: MoMA https://www.moma.org/collection/works/3239 (403 on fetch) and retailer pages [search-only]

### 24. Marion Bataille: "ABC3D" (2008)
- How it works: letters that transform through a limited set of paper-engineering moves: fold-ups, pull-tabs and undercuts. Reported mechanics: a mirror turns a V into a W, a translucent screen turns O into Q, C flips to D, E loses its arm to become F. Black, cream and red only.
- To a place card: a letter-transformation card, e.g. a pull-tab that turns the initial of the guest's name into the next letter, or a flap making "D" into the event word. Pure typographic play without blobby lettering.
- Sources: https://tarabooks.com/blog/10-time-and-movement-in-book-design/ [opened; confirms movement and time, but the specific mechanics are not stated there]; mechanics from search snippets of Seattle Review of Books [search-only]

### 25. Bruno Munari: "Libro illeggibile MN1" (first series 1949; Corraini edition 1984)
- How it works: 28 pages from 14 sheets, 100 x 100 mm, cut in various shapes and notched, bound with a single loop of red thread knotted at the spine. The colour, cut and sequence of pages replace text; reading is like playing a melody.
- To a place card: a small square booklet of die-cut lemon pages whose cuts make a rhythm; the name is revealed only at the last page. Handmade feel, and different from Munari travel sculptures already covered.
- Source: https://blogs.massart.edu/artistsbooks/?p=6600 [opened]

### 26. Warja Lavater: "Folded Stories" (accordion leporellos, 19 titles 1962-67; "William Tell" for MoMA, 1962)
- How it works: an accordion-folded single sheet that tells a story entirely in coloured abstract symbols, with a legend.
- To a place card: a concertina card that unfolds into a wordless symbol story of the evening (courses as signs), with the guest's name hidden in the legend. Folded flat for the table, then opened.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warja_Lavater [search-only]

### 27. Hideyuki Oka: "How to Wrap Five Eggs" (1967) and tsutsumi
- How it works: a catalogue of traditional Japanese wrappers and containers in bamboo, rice straw, hemp twine, paper and leaves; the form of the package comes from the thing wrapped.
- To a place card: a card that is a little wrapper (a folded envelope, twine or straw cord, a slot for a lemon slice or a spoon) and the guest opens it to find the name. Concept rather than a mechanism.
- Source: https://shop.japanhouselondon.uk/product/how-to-wrap-five-eggs/ [opened]

### 28. George Brecht: "Water Yam" (1963; published by Fluxus, designed by George Maciunas)
- How it works: a card-box of 69 offset event-score cards ("Chair Event", "Bed Event"). Each is a tiny instruction for an action.
- To a place card: each place card carries a short event-score instruction (e.g. "Turn the card over when the lights dim") that the table performs, so the card gives a behaviour rather than an object.
- Source: MoMA https://www.moma.org/collection/works/126322 (403 on fetch) [search-only]

### 29. Issey Miyake / Reality Lab: "IN-EI" lamps for Artemide (2010)
- How it works: each lamp is a single folded sheet of non-woven fabric from recycled PET bottles; it stands without a frame and stores flat. "In-ei" means shadow or nuance.
- To a place card: precedent for a single sheet folded to stand, glow and store flat, with the folds drawing the shadow. A reference for the fold geometry of a freestanding cone-like card. [search-only]
- Source: https://www.shift.jp.org/en/blog/2014/04/in-ei-issey-miyake [search-only]

## TOP 6 MECHANISMS FOR A MEMORABLE PLACE CARD (ranked)

1. SEAT-VIEWPOINT / TWO-WAY WORD (Raetz Yes/No + Varini + Agam pleats).
   Why first: the table already fixes everyone's eye position, so no extra prop is needed. The card reads the guest's name from their seat and something else (neighbour, "Dinner", a joke) from the opposite seat or from standing. It is a pure paper-and-print detail, ready for black-on-lemon, and unlike the poster (hard-edged, straight, not blobby). Build in two ways: a twisted folded strip (Raetz) or an accordion-pleated card with printing on facets only (Agam). Try a seated-height anamorphic print as an upgrade.
2. SHADOW TYPE UNDER THE LAMP (Eerdekens, Fukuda, Munari).
   Biggest wow and ties directly to the cone lamps, and the cutlery shadow (Fukuda) ties to dinner. Risk: cone lamps throw light down onto the table, not at a wall, and the angle varies per table. Mitigate with a design that works for a low raking shadow on the lemon table and a fallback (the card is still a good sculpture without light). Prototype under the actual lamp before choosing.
3. SELF-RIGHTING ROCKER (Kelly Rocker, Okiagari-koboshi, Gomboc).
   A behaviour, not a picture: nudge it and it wobbles and settles with the name facing the seat. A folded ellipse costs almost nothing; a weighted cone is a step up and echoes the lamp shape. Instantly understood without instructions.
4. HEXAFLEXAGON / HIDDEN FACES (Stone, Gardner; leporello cousin Lavater).
   A flat hexagon that flexes inside-out to reveal name, course, a question. Reveal-on-use with a known maths pedigree, lays flat, and graphically very strong in two colours. Needs an instruction hint (or discovered by play).
5. KIRIGAMI BUCKLING LATTICE (Rafsanjani and Bertoldi).
   Flat on arrival; pulled, it becomes a 3D lattice that lets light through. A real surprise, but depends on card stock and needs a stress test so it does not tear (and on the guest being willing to pull it).
6. CHAIN THAT BUILDS A LARGER WHOLE (Bouroullec Algues).
   Each card clips to its neighbour so the table forms one continuous shape; the form completes only when everyone sits. Strong social idea and not a poster echo, but fiddly tolerances, and an empty seat breaks the chain (design it so that it degrades gracefully).

Runner-ups: spinning disc (Duchamp Rotoreliefs / thaumatrope) is the cheapest "behaviour" and a good add-on to any of the above; paper-lithophane / vellum lamp-glow (Zettel'z) depends on a light source not guaranteed under black cone lamps; orosz-style mirror anamorph needs tableware that cannot be controlled.
