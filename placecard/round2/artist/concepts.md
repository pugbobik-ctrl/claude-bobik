# Dinner — Colorblock × DNA Kitchen · place card · Artist round 2

10 Oct 2026, Moscow. Black on lemon (#feed95), Wix Madefor Text (the Cyrillic glyphs are present in the supplied TTFs, so the printed copy below is Russian with the Latin names as the poster has them). Sample guest: **Sophia Zhuravkova**. Line-up used in the sketches: Tanya Andrianova, Igor Zotov, Andrey Lee, Sasha Chernikov, Sophia Zhuravkova.

None of these use the blobby lettering or receipt paper. Every card still carries the guest's name and marks the seat. Each answers one question: what does the card make people do to each other?

## Ranking

| # | Concept | Verb | Risk | Delight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Для тех, кого нет** / The Absent Guest | give a seat away | 3 | 5 |
| 2 | **Съешь имя** / Eat the Name | consume, swap | 4 | 5 |
| 3 | **Один тост на всех** / One Toast | speak as one | 2 | 4 |
| 4 | **Партитура на пять перемен** / Score for Five Courses | perform, tear | 2 | 4 |
| 5 | **Круговая порука** / The Travelling Portrait | pass, write, deliver | 3 | 4 |
| 6 | **Подсвети говорящего** / Light the Speaker | light each other | 4 | 4 |

Ranking logic: 1 has the most meaning and uses the one prop the table already has (the lamps). 2 has the most surprise and ties the lemon colour to food. 3 and 4 are the safest, and 3 is the cleverest. 5 and 6 are good, but 5 needs pencils and shy guests, and 6 needs a light test.

Compatible pairs worth showing the client: **1 + 3** (the absent tab on one side, the toast line on the other; one card, two rituals) or **2 alone**, as the single "wow" object.

Files: `c1.png … c6.png` (rendered sheets, 1800×1260) and `c1.svg … c6.svg` (editable). Generator scripts are in `src/`. Each sheet has the object in a table scene (top left), a flat print layout (top right) and a four-panel storyboard (bottom).

## 1. Для тех, кого нет — The Absent Guest
*Sheet: `c1.png`*

**Guest experience.** Sophia arrives to a lemon house with her name, and next to it a small perforated tab: «Кого здесь нет?» (Who isn't here?). Between the first courses a short pencil, which is already at every place, makes sense of it. She writes one name on the tab: a friend in another city, someone who has died, an ex, a dog, nobody. She tears the tab off and stands it against the foot of the nearest lamp. By dessert every lamp has a small crowd of names around it, so the lamps have become seats for the people who couldn't come. When leaving, she photographs her lamp and sends it to the person on her tab. The back of the tab says «Мы о тебе думали» (We thought of you), so the photo carries the message.

**Printed where.**
- Front, name panel: «Dinner · 10.10.2026», big `Sophia / Zhuravkova`.
- Front, tab: «Кого здесь нет?» plus a writing line.
- Back, facing the guest: her name small, then «Напиши на язычке имя того, кого за этим столом не хватает. Оторви. Поставь у лампы.»
- Back of the tab: «Мы о тебе думали.»

**Object.** A tent (A-frame) card with the ridge on top. The left 40 mm is a perforated tab that is itself a tiny tent and stands alone when torn off, so it can lean on a lamp foot.

**Materials / size / production.**
- 140×90 mm flat, folds to 140×45.
- 300 gsm lemon card, one colour (black), die-cut.
- Score along the ridge, perforate (3 mm bridge) along the tab.
- One short black pencil per place.
- 30 guests means 30 cards, 30 pencils and about 90 minutes of cutting.

**Risk 3.** It is sentimental, and a table may find "someone not here" heavy. Mitigation: the prompt allows any name and any tone, and the host can model it by writing a dog's name first.

**Delight 5.** It is the only concept that gives the lamps a meaning and leaves a physical trace of who was missed, and the photo-to-the-absent-person step carries the evening out of the room.

## 2. Съешь имя — Eat the Name
*Sheet: `c2.png`*

**Guest experience.** On the plate lies a lemon disc with her name, like a yolk. Everyone half-asks whether it's edible. With the first course the waiter lifts the disc to the napkin, and it sits on the table like a small moon. At tea, guests swap discs with a neighbour. The rule: only someone else's name may be eaten. The hot tea softens the wafer, the black letters loosen and float, and the name dissolves in your cup. She leaves with Andrey inside her, and he with Sophia.

**Printed where.**
- Disc centre: `Sophia / Zhuravkova`.
- Ring around the edge: «DINNER · 10.10.2026 · COLORBLOCK × DNA KITCHEN ·».
- No other text. The rule is spoken by the waiter or printed on the menu.

**Object.** A flat disc lying on the plate, then on the napkin.

**Materials / size / production.**
- Ø130 mm, lemon-tinted wafer (rice or potato-starch) paper 0.3 mm, printed with edible black ink on a food printer.
- Six discs fit on an A4 sheet.
- Make 1–2 days ahead, store flat in a sealed box with silica gel, lay out about 20 minutes before guests arrive.
- Wafer paper is sold by cake-decorating suppliers in Moscow; a sugar icing sheet is the alternative.
- The lemon will not match #feed95 exactly. Test a swatch.

**Risk 4.**
- Food-handling rules and the venue's tolerance for an edible place card.
- The wafer curls in humid rooms and near hot plates.
- Edible black is dark grey, not a pure black.
- Some guests will refuse to eat paper.
- This is the only concept that is not "black on lemon card", so it needs client sign-off.

**Delight 5.** It is the most surprising object and the most Vogelzang. You literally digest the person you sat next to, and the lemon colour becomes a taste memory.

## 3. Один тост на всех — One Toast
*Sheet: `c5.png`*

**Guest experience.** Her name faces the table. On the guest's side is printed one line in large type: «За тех, кто сидит рядом.» She doesn't know it is the end of a toast. At dessert someone stands, taps a glass and reads their line. Then each person reads theirs, in seat order, and the whole table speaks one sentence: «Пусть этот стол будет длиннее, чем мы рассчитывали, а свет над ним — ниже, чем нужно, чтобы нам пришлось придвинуться. За тех, кто сидит рядом.» Sophia gets the last line. She keeps the card, which is now the only copy of her line.

**Printed where.**
- Face, outward: «Dinner · 10.10.2026», large name.
- Back, facing the guest: her name small, then the line in 30 pt.
- Line breaks and the lower-case starts of lines 2–5 hint at the order without numbering.

**Object.** A tent card. For tables larger than five, write the toast in verses of five and make every fifth card «Все: за нас!».

**Materials / size / production.**
- 110×60 mm tent, flat 110×120.
- 300 gsm lemon card, one colour, score on the ridge.
- Variant for brave hosts: hand out the cards in random order and the guests seat themselves into grammatical order; the seating is the sentence.
- The text has to be rewritten per headcount (the five lines above are for the line-up).

**Risk 2.** The cheapest and most robust concept. Shy readers are protected by the chorus format: each person says only one line.

**Delight 4.** A clean idea: the table is one text and nobody owns it. It also turns the lamps into the toast's subject («свет ниже, чем нужно»).

## 4. Партитура на пять перемен — Score for Five Courses
*Sheet: `c3.png`*

**Guest experience.** The card shows her name and, below it, one instruction on the top sheet of a small tear-off pad. When the plates change she tears the sheet and does what it says. The five sheets, in order:
1. «Расскажи соседу слева, где был год назад.»
2. «Передай соль. Никто не просил.»
3. «Услышишь тишину — присоединись к ней.»
4. «Пересядь на 10 минут. Бокал возьми с собой.»
5. «Попрощайся, будто ужин только начинается.»

She can't see the next one, which is the surprise. Sheet 3 is a silence that nobody starts: whoever tears it first goes quiet, the rest hear it and join. Under the last sheet, printed on the card itself: «Дальше — без инструкций.» The torn sheets stay on the cloth as lemon confetti.

**Printed where.**
- Front: «Dinner», the name, then the pad of five 92×44 mm sheets.
- Under the pad, printed on the card: «Дальше — без инструкций».
- Back: name, «Меняют тарелки — рви листок. Исполнил — оставь на столе.», venue and date.

**Object.** A tent card with a small pad glued to its lower front, like a desk tear-off calendar.

**Materials / size / production.**
- Card 100×86 mm (flat 100×172), 350 gsm.
- Pad: five sheets 92×44 mm on 100 gsm lemon paper, padded (gummed) along the top edge.
- Print five sheet designs plus the card.
- Padding by a print shop; 30 pads is cheap.

**Risk 2.** Guests may ignore it. The failure is graceful: the card is still a name card. Instruction no. 4 asks for a seating shuffle, so check it with the venue.

**Delight 4.** It is the most direct Fluxus gesture (an event score at the table). The pad format keeps the next instruction hidden, and the final printed line gives it a punchline.

## 5. Круговая порука — The Travelling Portrait
*Sheet: `c4.png`*

**Guest experience.** The tent keeps her seat. A narrow slip printed with her name stands in a slot in its front. After the first course every slip travels one seat to the left. The neighbour who now holds Sophia's slip writes one word under a printed prompt: «первое впечатление», «цвет», «подарок», «недосказанное», «пожелание». By dessert her slip has five lines of other people's handwriting. Before leaving, everyone finds the owner of the slip they hold and hands it over. She gets a portrait of herself by the table.

**Printed where.**
- Tent front: name. Tent back: «После каждой перемены передай полоску соседу слева. Допиши на ней одно слово по подсказке. В конце найди хозяина — и вручи.»
- Slip: name at the top, then five prompts, each over a writing line.

**Object.** A tent with a die-cut slot (54 mm) and a separate slip, 50×120 mm, with a 25 mm tab that sits in the slot.

**Materials / size / production.**
- Tent 120×50 mm, 350 gsm lemon, one slot.
- Slip 50×120 mm, 350 gsm lemon, one colour.
- One short black pencil per place.

**Risk 3.** The ritual relies on pencils and on guests being willing to write about each other. The prompts are deliberately gentle and one-word. On a long table the travel distance must be capped at five seats.

**Delight 4.** What you keep is other people's handwriting about you. The delivery at the end moves people around the table.

## 6. Подсвети говорящего — Light the Speaker
*Sheet: `c6.png`*

**Guest experience.** On the card there is a round mirror window above her name. She turns it, and a bright spot runs across the ceiling: the first smile. Then someone starts to speak, and the neighbour turns their card to catch the table lamp and lays the spot on the speaker's face. The back of the card says, «Тому, кто говорит, нужен свет. Поймай лампу — и подари её ему. Только не в глаза.» Over the evening everyone lights everyone. At the toast all the cards aim at one point above the table, and the guests take the mirror home with their name on it.

**Printed where.**
- Front: round window Ø45 mm, name below it.
- Back: name small, then the instruction above.

**Object.** A leaning tent whose front has a die-cut window with mirror board glued behind it.

**Materials / size / production.**
- Tent 110×50 mm (flat 110×100), 350 gsm lemon.
- Window Ø45 mm, mirror board (metallised card, laminated) 56×56 mm glued inside.
- One print colour.
- The mirror can't be printed lemon, so it stays silver.

**Risk 4.**
- The geometry of black cone lamps, tilt angle and mirror size must be tested under the real lamps; the lamps throw light mostly downward.
- Glare in eyes.
- Mirror board scratches in transit.
- It can read as a gadget if no one gets the idea.

**Delight 4.** The visual surprise is strong and everyone gets to be both the light and the lit face.

## Notes for the client conversation

- All six keep the name large and legible at a glance, and the card stands at the seat; any of them can also carry an unmistakable "Dinner · 10.10.2026".
- All printed Russian text is a first draft in the voice of the host; wording should be signed off by the client before print.
- Concepts 1, 3, 4 and 5 are pure card; 2 is food, 6 adds mirror board.
- If the client wants one decision: **1 (Absent Guest)** as the main card, with **3 (One Toast)** line printed on its back, and **2 (Eat the Name)** reserved for the dessert plate if the venue's kitchen agrees.
